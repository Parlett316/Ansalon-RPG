POI W "A Broken Tower" A stub of a tower leans out of the mire, its upper third sheared away centuries ago. Moss and standing water have claimed whatever carvings once marked it as someone's pride.
POI V "A Broken Tower" Half a tower rises from the ruin across the plaza, its stonework blackened as if by old fire. Water drips steadily from somewhere near its jagged top, though the sky above is clear.
POI C "The Old Well Shaft" A wide masonry shaft drops away into darkness at the center of the ruin, its stone lip worn smooth by centuries of rain. Whatever it once drew water from lies far below now, lost with the rest of the city.
POI H "The Hanging Chain" A huge iron chain descends out of the mist overhead and vanishes into the ruin below, its far ends lost in both directions. No one currently alive built it, and nothing about its size suggests it was meant for anything human-sized.
POI F "A Waterfall" Runoff from the swamp above spills over a broken ledge in a steady curtain, feeding the pools that have swallowed half the old streets. The sound of it carries over everything else here.
POI S "A Ruin-Scavenger" A stranger crouches near the well shaft's rim, sorting through a satchel of things pulled up from below -- shards, coins gone black, nothing whole. They don't look surprised to have company; people who come to Xak Tsaroth rarely come for the view.
TALK S "Most of what's down there's worth less than the climb," they say, not looking up. "But I found one thing I can't use." They hold up a slim rod capped in tarnished gold and silver, a clouded crystal window set into its length. "Priest's work, old as the temples that used to stand here. Doesn't answer to my hands at all. Maybe it'll answer to yours."
TALK_AFTER S The scavenger doesn't look up right away, but the pause before they speak says they remember. "Had company here, a while back. Eight strangers, tramping through my ruins like they owned the water table." They turn a shard over in their fingers, considering. "One of them coughed something awful the whole while, tracing the old carvings like he could read them -- maybe he could, for all I know. Big one stuck close, sword loose, watching every shadow like it owed him money. Dwarf gave the stonework an honest look, at least -- more than most bother." A short, dry huff. "And a kender went down a stairwell before anyone could stop him. Came back up grinning, hoopak first, like he'd found the whole city just for himself." They set the shard down. "Didn't take anything of mine, if that's what you're wondering. Wish I could say the same for half the 'customers' I get through here."
