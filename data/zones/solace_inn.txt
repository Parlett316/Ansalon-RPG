POI K "The Bar" Otik Sandeth works the taps himself, sleeves rolled up, while Tika Waylan ducks between tables with a loaded tray and a look daring anyone to complain about the wait.
POI O "Otik Sandeth" Up close, the innkeeper has the look of a man who's heard every excuse for an unpaid tab there is -- and forgiven most of them anyway.
POI Y "Tika Waylan" She balances a full tray on one hand, sizing you up with the quick, practical eye of someone who's learned to spot trouble before it reaches a table.
POI F "The Great Fireplace" A fire big enough to roast a boar crackles in a stone hearth built straight into the vallenwood's trunk. Regulars claim it has never once gone out.
POI C "A Corner Table" Deep gouges scar the tabletop, like something with an axe once made a point of testing its edge here. Half the regulars have a story about who did it, and no two stories agree.
POI D "Otik's Kitchen" Through the doorway, the smell of frying spiced potatoes is almost a physical wall. Someone is singing off-key over the clatter of pans.
POI U "The Stairs Up" A narrow stair spirals up around the trunk toward the Inn's private rooms, lit by lanterns hung from the branches above.
TALK O Otik wipes down the bar without looking up. "Sit, sit -- you look like you've walked further than you meant to. First mug's on the house, if you've got a good story to trade for it."
TALK_AGAIN O Otik nods you toward your usual spot without asking. "Back again. I'll not pretend I remember the story you owed me, but I remember you owing one. Settle up whenever you like."
TALK_AFTER O Otik sets down the rag he's been wiping the bar with, and for a moment doesn't look like a man running a tavern -- just a man missing some faces he's known a long while. "Caramon and that brother of his, I fed since they were both knee-high, if you can credit that of the big one now. Sturm grew up right here same as the twins did, whatever else changed about him since. Kender's been lifting spoons off these tables since before he could see over one. Tanis and Flint I've known nearly as long, though they came to us grown, not raised in the place." He shakes his head slowly. "Five years gone, the lot of them, off chasing some oath they wouldn't talk about -- and true to their word, they came back through that door. Thinner. Grimmer. Dragging along a Plainswoman and her silent fellow I'd never once laid eyes on before." A faint, fond shrug. "Settled every tab without me asking, same as they always did -- that part, at least, hadn't changed. Stayed a night or two, then gone again. Some say toward Haven. Others swear they cut through Darken Wood instead, which if it's true means five years hasn't made that lot any wiser." He picks the rag back up. "Either way, I mean to see them all through that door again someday. Steel or no steel owed."
SAY_IF O kender Otik's grin turns rueful before you've said a word. "Ah -- one of your kind. I'll save us both the trouble: house rules say I remember every unpaid tab, whether or not you remember owing it. That said, first mug's still on the house. I've never once regretted the policy. Mostly."
TOPIC O "Keeping the Peace" Otik lowers his voice, glancing toward the door out of old habit. "You learn to keep peace behind a bar, and that goes twofold once the Highseekers start sending their own kind through on Theocracy business. Even a theocrat needs his belly filled and the fire and brimstone washed out of his throat by the end of a long day of preaching -- and I've never once turned away a coin for who's holding it. Bad for business, having opinions where the wrong ears can hear them."
SUBJECT O tab,debt,unpaid,mug First mug's on the house for anyone with a story worth the trade -- always has been, long as I've kept this bar. Doesn't mean I forget who still owes me a second one. Bad for business, forgetting. Worse for business, saying so out loud.
SUBJECT O kender,kenders Can't run an inn on the Solace road and not learn to live with kender walking through the door same as anyone else. Lost count of what's wandered off in a kender's pocket by accident over the years. Never once been worth the argument. Cheaper to replace a spoon than a regular.
SUBJECT_UNKNOWN O Otik shrugs, still wiping the bar. "Ask me something I can pour you an answer to. That one's past my reach."
