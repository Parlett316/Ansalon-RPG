POI H "The Harbor" White-winged ships ride at anchor along a stone-walled quay, more of them idle than working -- a port that remembers being busier, and safer, than it is now.
POI L "The Lord's Keep" A blunt stone hall overlooking the bazaar, banners of Kalaman snapping from its battlements. Its steps still smell faintly of trampled winter roses, though no one seems to remember why anyone would have carried flowers up them.
POI W "A Stair to the City Wall" Stone steps climb to the wall-walk above, worn smooth in a straight line toward the northern approach. On a good day, children race each other up it for the view. On a bad one, the whole city stands here in silence, watching the plain.
POI C "A Cartographer's Stall" A locked wooden stall crammed with rolled charts, its hinges cheap enough to worry any honest lock. A hand-lettered sign reads "CLOSED," optimistically.
POI M "Market Square" An open-air bazaar spills across the square in gaily-colored booths -- vendors, games of chance, the occasional dancing bear when the crowds are thick enough to draw one. Even quiet, it never quite loses the smell of frying bacon and cheap wine.
POI G "A City Watchman" He stands near the market's edge, one hand resting on a truncheon worn more from boredom than use, eyes moving over the crowd the way a man watches for pickpockets he already knows he won't catch.
TALK G "Mind your purse in the market, stranger -- we get more than our share of light fingers when the bazaar's running. Rest of the city's honest enough. Mostly."
TALK_AGAIN G "Still watching the crowd, still not catching whoever's been at the purses today. Some things don't change."
TALK_AFTER G The watchman's easy manner cools somewhere deeper than usual, the way it does when a memory outweighs the joke he'd rather make. "Had a strange spring here, stranger than most. Dignitaries in the streets, some kind of homecoming -- a dwarf stuffed into dress armor looking like he'd rather be anywhere else, a golden-haired woman riding the parade route like she'd been drafted into it." A short, humorless laugh. "And somewhere in all that, a kender picked the lock on that cartographer's stall over there slicker than anything I've caught in this market in ten years. Pouches fuller than his conscience, if he's even got one." The humor drains out entirely. "Came back through later, that same lot -- a whole crowd of them this time, all of Kalaman standing at the wall with them, watching the north like the sky itself owed us an answer." He's quiet a moment. "Don't know what became of all of them after. Don't know if I want to."
TOPIC G "The War's Turn" The watchman's easy manner cools. "You'll have heard the news same as anyone -- a Golden General, dragonlances, silver and gold dragons driving the evil ones out of the sky. Good news, all of it. I'd feel better about it if I didn't keep noticing how many soldiers we're still burying."
SUBJECT G cartographer,stall Been locked up tight as long as I've walked this beat. Whatever's behind that door, the owner trusts the lock more than the neighborhood -- and given what I see most days in this market, can't say I blame him.
SUBJECT G harbor,ships,port Harbor remembers being busier. Safer, too, if the old-timers are to be believed. These days half those ships ride at anchor doing nothing, which tells you plenty about how the war's going without me saying a word of it.
SUBJECT_UNKNOWN G The watchman shrugs, eyes still on the crowd. "Wouldn't know. Ask somebody who isn't paid to watch pockets, not answer questions."
