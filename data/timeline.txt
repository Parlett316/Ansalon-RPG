# data/timeline.txt -- hand-authored canon character schedule for the
# chance-encounter system. Grammar: docs/TIMELINE_NOTES.md. All eight
# Heroes of the Lance share one schedule through Dragons of Autumn Twilight
# (Solace -> Haven/Darken Wood -> Xak Tsaroth -> Qualinesti -> Pax Tharkas
# -> Tarsis), then diverge at Tarsis as of Dragons of Winter Night: Sturm,
# Flint, and Tasslehoff continue on, first to Ice Wall Castle (a
# dragon-orb quest, reachable only by the sea-travel mechanic -- see
# GameState::hasBoat / docs/ARCHITECTURE.md) and then to the High Clerist's
# Tower, while Tanis, Raistlin, Caramon, Goldmoon, and Riverwind are swept
# east to Silvanesti instead, griffon-carried into a second dragon-orb
# crisis of their own -- see docs/TIMELINE_NOTES.md's "Dragons of Winter
# Night"/"Ice Wall"/"Silvanesti" sections for the sourcing. Original prose
# throughout, not quoted from the books. More
# locations are meant to be added the same incremental way zones were.
#
# CHARACTER <id>          starts a block
# NAME <text>              display name
# PRESENCE <location-id> <day-start> <day-end> <text>   one or more
# SAY <text>                optional, follows a PRESENCE line -- the
#                           default/first-meeting spoken line shown when
#                           the player presses 't' during that window
# SAY_IF <condition> <text> optional, zero or more; a reactive variant of
#                           SAY, checked in order the first time this
#                           window is talked to -- the first condition
#                           that matches the player's character wins over
#                           the plain SAY. Conditions: good, evil (align-
#                           ment's ethical axis), human/dwarf/elf/gnome/
#                           halfelf/halfling/kender (race), fighter/mage/
#                           cleric/thief/tinker (class)
# SAY_AGAIN <text>          optional; shown on every talk after the first,
#                           instead of the generic recognition fallback
# TOPIC "<label>" <text>    optional, zero or more; if any exist, a topic
#                           picker follows the greeting -- label is quoted
#                           (see docs/TIMELINE_NOTES.md)
# END                      closes the block

PRESENCE solace 0 1 A half-elf with a neatly trimmed beard sits near the fire, turning a plain ring over and over in his fingers, lost in thought he clearly doesn't want to explain.
SAY Tanis glances up, startled out of thought, then recovers with an easy half-smile. "Sorry -- I didn't mean to stare through you. It's been a strange five years. You look like you've got your own road behind you; mind if I don't ask about it, and you don't ask about mine?"
SAY_IF halfelf Tanis's easy half-smile falters for a moment into something rawer. "You're -- " He stops, recalculates, tries again. "Forgive me. I don't often meet another of us. I spent most of my life thinking I was the only word of that particular language anyone spoke."
SAY_IF elf Tanis's eyes flick to your ears, your bearing, and something in his posture straightens -- old habit, old caution. "An elf, this far from Qualinesti. There's a story there, I'd guess. There usually is, with us." He says "us" like he isn't entirely sure he's allowed to.
SAY_AGAIN Tanis's guarded half-smile comes easier this time. "Back again. Good -- I find I'm glad of a face that isn't asking me hard questions about the last five years."
TOPIC "The True Gods" Tanis's expression turns inward. "I spent five years walking half of Ansalon looking for some sign the old gods hadn't abandoned us -- a cleric, a shrine, anything. I found silence, mostly. Then Goldmoon walked into the Inn carrying a staff that glows blue and heals the dying, and now I don't know what to believe. I'm not sure that's better."
PRESENCE haven 2 3 Tanis stands at the edge of the market, arguing quietly but firmly with a farmer about which road is safest north -- he doesn't look like a man used to losing that argument.
SAY Tanis breaks off his argument long enough to nod at you. "If you're heading north, take the eastern track, not the river road -- I've heard too many stories about the river road lately. Call it caution. I've gotten better at listening to it."
SAY_AGAIN Tanis gives you a brief nod across the market without breaking off his conversation. "Still arguing about the roads. Some things don't change in five years, either."
TOPIC "The Highseekers' Promise" Tanis's easy manner falters. "It was Tika's idea, first -- she believed the Highseekers here might still care about truth, whatever's rotted at Solace. I let myself hope she was right, because the alternative was admitting we might not have anywhere better to send people. Sturm asked me once what we'd do if the Highseekers here turned out just as bad as the one we left behind." He doesn't quite finish the thought. "I still don't have a good answer for that. I keep meaning to find one before it stops being hypothetical."
PRESENCE xak_tsaroth 4 6 Half-buried in the ruined causeways, Tanis crouches at the water's edge, studying something small and metal in his palm before tucking it away.
SAY Tanis closes his hand around whatever he's holding before you get a good look. "Old ruins keep old secrets. Best not to go digging in them alone -- I'd rather not learn that lesson twice."
SAY_AGAIN Tanis glances up from whatever he's studying, unbothered. "Ruins keep their secrets whether you're here once or a dozen times. I'm still working on mine."
PRESENCE qualinesti 7 9 Tanis walks these living halls like a man half a stranger, half at home -- the elves here watch him with an attention they don't spare for other outsiders.
SAY Tanis manages a tired, genuine smile. "Half of me still expects someone to ask me to leave. The other half never left at all. It's... complicated, being home."
SAY_AGAIN Tanis's smile is a little less tired than before. "Still half a stranger here. Getting used to the feeling, though. That's something."
PRESENCE darken_wood 2 3 Tanis stands at the tree line with a hand still resting on his sword hilt, the look of a man mentally re-tallying a decision he's already made and can't unmake.
SAY Tanis manages a rueful smile. "I got us into this wood. I'd rather not spend the walk out explaining why. Ask Raistlin, if you want a real answer -- he's the one who was certain about the name of this place, not me."
SAY_AGAIN Tanis glances back at the tree line, still faintly rueful. "No one's blamed me for this wood yet today. I'll take it."
PRESENCE pax_tharkas 10 12 Tanis stands near the fortress gates counting heads without seeming to, the particular tension of a man who knows exactly how many people he's responsible for getting out alive.
SAY Tanis doesn't take his eyes off the gate mechanism overhead. "We're not out of this yet. Watch the walls, not the view -- Pax Tharkas has a way of finishing conversations early."
SAY_AGAIN Tanis's eyes are still on the walls, not you. "Still counting heads. Ask me again once we're clear of this place."
PRESENCE tarsis 20 22 Tanis stands near the harbor's dry, dust-choked mooring rings, one hand resting on his sword hilt without seeming to notice he's put it there -- a half-elf who has learned to read a city's mood before he's finished reading its streets.
SAY Tanis's eyes don't quite settle anywhere long. "Tarsis makes for strange lodging. A harbor with no water, and a city that still argues about where it went. Keep your coin close and your voice down -- I don't like how quickly the guards noticed us."
SAY_AGAIN Tanis's gaze is still moving, still counting exits without meaning to. "Still watching the guards. Old habit. I'd rather have it and not need it."
TOPIC "A City Without a Sea" Tanis studies the beached hulls along the old harbor with something between pity and unease. "Tarsis lost its sea to the Cataclysm same as Ansalon lost half of everything else, and never got around to admitting it. I understand a city that won't stop arguing about what it lost. I've been having a version of that argument with myself for five years."
PRESENCE silvanesti 25 30 Tanis stands near the Ferry Landing with his arms crossed, watching the white Tower of the Stars across the water with the guarded expression of a man who trusts almost nothing about how they got here, least of all his own relief at arriving.
SAY "Raistlin knew what waited across that river before we ever crossed it," Tanis says, low, not looking away from the tower. "He won't say how, and I've stopped asking in front of the others. I haven't stopped wondering." His hand doesn't leave his sword hilt.
SAY_AGAIN Tanis is still watching the tower, still not entirely trusting the quiet around it. "Nothing's changed since I last said this to you. I don't find that reassuring in a place like this."
SAY_IF halfelf Tanis's guarded look toward the tower softens, just slightly, at the sight of you. "Strange, being the only half-elf most rooms have ever held, and then walking into the one land that would call both halves of me unwelcome for different reasons. I don't say that looking for sympathy. I say it because I suspect you'd understand it without needing it explained."
TOPIC "A Half-Elf's Doubt" Tanis's jaw sets. "I've trusted Raistlin with my life more times than I can count, and distrusted his reasons for helping me nearly as often. Both of those are still true standing here. I don't know what he traded, or who he traded it with, to know what he knew. I only know he hasn't told me, and that frightens me more than the dragon did."
PRESENCE kalaman 100 100 Tanis stands at the base of the city wall among a silent crowd, jaw tight, his hand never leaving his sword hilt as he watches the northern sky for a shape he already knows is coming.
SAY Tanis's voice is low, rough with a grief he hasn't had time to set down yet. "Flint told me about Sturm. And about who came for Laurana -- the Golden General, everyone's calling her now -- and why." He doesn't elaborate on the why. "I've made mistakes that cost other people before. I don't know how to make this one cost only me."
SAY_AGAIN Tanis is still watching the northern sky, still not at ease. "The waiting hasn't gotten any easier. I don't expect it to, before this is finished."
TOPIC "A Debt He Won't Name" Tanis's expression doesn't change, but something in his voice tightens. "Gilthanas holds me responsible for whatever happens to her, and he isn't wrong to. I let someone close who I had no business trusting, and she used exactly that against all of us." He's quiet a moment. "I don't ask you to tell me it isn't my fault. I'd rather earn my way back to deserving her trust than be told I already have it."
PRESENCE godshome 103 103 Tanis kneels in the dust with blood dried dark on his hands, staring at nothing, a half-elf who has just learned the difference between what he saw and what was actually there.
SAY "I put a sword through a man who was trying to save him, Flint," Tanis says, his voice hollow, not quite looking at you. "Berem got up afterward. Walked, talked, bled clean. I don't -- I don't know what he is. I know what I am, though, for about ten seconds back there. I'd rather not learn that again."
SAY_AGAIN Tanis's hands are steadier now, but his eyes still go to that stretch of bare stone more than they should. "I keep thinking there was a better ten seconds available to me back there. I haven't found it yet."
TOPIC "The Man Who Wouldn't Die" Tanis's jaw tightens. "He's called the Everman. Sturm and I watched him die once already, buried under a mountain of rock at Pax Tharkas. He got up from that, too." He shakes his head slowly. "Whatever he is, he's the only road left into Neraka, and I very nearly ended it with my own hand over a misunderstanding. I don't know whether to be grateful he can't be killed, or afraid of what that means."
PRESENCE neraka 105 107 Tanis stands near the ruin of the Temple gate with his arm around Laurana, watching the sky go back to being only sky. He looks like a man who spent the night doing arithmetic with people's lives and hasn't yet decided whether the sum came out even.
SAY Tanis's arm doesn't leave Laurana's side, even now. "I found Tas's things scattered on a dungeon floor, soaked through. I was certain -- " He stops, swallows it down. "He's alive. Caramon too. I don't know what I did to deserve getting to keep both of those true. I mean to spend a while being grateful instead of asking."
SAY_AGAIN Tanis still hasn't quite stopped watching the sky, like he expects the citadels to come back if he looks away too long. "It gets easier by the hour. Not easy. Easier." A faint, tired half-smile. "I'll take it."
TOPIC "The Man Who Finally Rested" Tanis is quiet a moment before he answers. "Berem's dead. Really dead, this time -- I watched it happen, after everything, though I wasn't the one who did it." His jaw works. "I spent weeks thinking his not-dying was a mercy or a curse depending on the hour. Turns out it was neither. It was just unfinished. He finished it himself, in the end, reaching for a sister he'd lost long before any of us ever met him." A long pause. "I hope wherever he ended up, she was there waiting."
TOPIC "A Ring of Gold and Steel" Tanis turns something invisible over between his fingers, an old habit even with nothing left to turn. "I offered her back the ring she once gave me. She threw it off the ridge instead, into the dark, without a second look." A tired, rueful almost-smile. "Told me it was an undisciplined heart that gave it the first time, and she's done being that. Said maybe someday she'll give me one that isn't just ivy leaves. Gold and steel, this time, she says." He looks north, toward a city still finishing its own war. "I don't know yet what a ring like that's supposed to mean. I mean to find out, at her side, in Kalaman, before I let myself worry about anything past that."
TOPIC "What Kitiara Asked For" Tanis's expression hardens. "She presented Laurana to the Dark Queen herself, called her a gift, said it plain in front of the whole Hall -- straight to the Death Chambers, was the idea. I hadn't known. I'd thought I understood the plan we were playing at." He stops, swallows. "Then she asked one more favor for herself: Laurana's soul, handed over to Lord Soth, some old debt of his against an elfwoman I don't think was ever her. I called her a traitor to her face, and she told me to shut up or ruin everything." A pause. "I still don't fully understand what she was playing at that night. I only know that whatever plan I thought we had, saving Laurana was never actually part of it -- not until I made it one myself, with a sword through a man's heart."
TOPIC "The Crown of Power" Tanis's jaw sets, remembering. "Something I never saw whispered in my ear to strike, right as I knelt to lay my sword at Ariakas's feet. So I put it through his heart instead -- he was a magic-user besides, by rights that blade should never have touched him." He's quiet a moment. "The Crown was still rolling on the floor when Lord Soth came for it himself. I held it over my head and swore I'd smash it before handing it to a dead man. Kitiara called him off, and had him walk me through that whole hostile hall instead -- safest escort I've ever had, and the worst company." A pause. "Then she wanted me to place it on her own head. I raised it up and set it on mine instead, told her plainly what I meant to do, and swore on Sturm and Flint both that she'd have it back once Laurana and I were clear -- if she kept her word." His voice goes flat. "Never found out if she meant to. Laurana came back to herself before Kitiara got the chance either way -- put a blade to my throat and threw the both of us off that platform. Crown went skidding loose into a hall full of Highlords already drawing steel over who'd rule next." A long pause. "I still don't know if Kitiara would have kept her promise. Kitiara was never only one thing. That's most of why I loved her, and most of why I couldn't stay."
END

CHARACTER raistlin
NAME Raistlin Majere

SUBJECT caramon,brother Raistlin's gaze drifts, briefly and unguarded, to the broad-shouldered man working through a mountain of food at the corner table. "My twin. My other half, if you credit that sort of thing." The half-smile that follows has real warmth buried under the dryness. "He worries over me the way some men worry over a favorite hound -- loudly, constantly, and mostly without being asked. I have told him it does neither of us any good." A pause. "He has never once listened. I confess I would not know what to do with him if he started."
SUBJECT magic,test,towers,sorcery,tower Raistlin's thin hand drifts, briefly, to his own hooded face. "I passed the Test, at the Towers of High Sorcery. I will not describe to you what it cost -- you have not earned that answer, and I find explaining myself to the uninitiated tiresome." His golden eyes glitter, sharp with old, private pain. "I will say only that I paid for this in a currency I expect to go on missing for the rest of my life. It was, on the whole, a fair trade."
SUBJECT kitiara,kit,sister,half-sister Raistlin's expression cools by several careful degrees. "Our half-sister. A complicated word for what she has actually become to us, but I have yet to find a better one." A thin, humorless smile. "Kitiara answers to whichever banner currently profits her most -- I do not pretend to know which one that is today, only that she will have already worked out the exchange rate before she chooses it." His golden eyes flick away. "Ask Caramon, if you want someone who still hopes for a different answer. I stopped some years ago."
SUBJECT eyes,hourglass,pupils,sight Raistlin's golden eyes glitter, distant for a moment. "The Test left me these -- hourglass pupils, so that I might see time itself, the way it wears away everything it touches." His thin mouth curves, humorless. "I see you dying, slowly, by inches, the same as I see everyone. It does not make company more pleasant. It has, admittedly, made me considerably harder to lie to."
SUBJECT skin,gold,golden Raistlin holds one thin, golden-hued hand up to the firelight without being asked, turning it as though he still finds the color a mild curiosity. "A parting gift from the Towers, along with the eyes. It does not wash off, in case you were wondering, and no, I have stopped minding the way people stare. Mostly." A thin smile. "It has its uses. Very little about me reads as harmless anymore, and I have found that worth quite a lot."
SUBJECT wayreth,conclave Raistlin's expression turns momentarily distant. "The Tower of Wayreth does not stay where it was the day before -- it moves through the forest at its own pleasure, and finds you rather than the reverse, if the Conclave judges you worth finding at all." A dry note creeps into his voice. "Three orders, one Tower, and a great many old wizards who would each prefer their own robe's color ran the world. I have found it wiser to let them believe they might."
SUBJECT staff,magius Raistlin's hand finds the plain-looking staff never far from his side, something almost fond in the gesture. "Par-Salian's own parting gift, given the day I passed the Test. It looks like a beggar's walking stick, which I have found to be its own kind of protection -- very few men draw a blade on a cripple leaning on a stick, right up until they regret it." His golden eyes glint. "I would not travel far from it, if I were you. I certainly don't."
SUBJECT par-salian,parsalian,salian Raistlin's voice cools, respect and old resentment tangled together in it. "Par-Salian is head of my order, and the one who judged me fit to attempt the Test at an age when most mages are still copying their masters' scrolls by candlelight. He warned me what it might cost. I do not believe, even now, that either of us fully believed the number he named." A thin pause. "He was not wrong to let me try. I have not yet decided whether he was right."
SUBJECT robes,order,orders,neutral,neutrality Raistlin's red robes shift as he inclines his head, very slightly. "White for the well-meaning, black for the ones who stopped pretending, and red for those of us who decided the argument between them wasn't worth having." His thin smile doesn't warm. "I chose neutrality with my eyes open, not because I lack conviction, but because I have watched conviction get a great many capable people killed for the comfort of feeling righteous about it."
SUBJECT moons,moon,solinari,lunitari,nuitari Raistlin's golden eyes lift briefly toward a sky where, tonight, you can't actually see them. "Three moons govern the Art, whether or not you can find them overhead. Solinari answers the White Robes, black Nuitari answers to no one polite enough to admit it, and red Lunitari answers mine." A thin, private smile. "Mine also happens to be the only one you can see plainly on most nights. Make of that whatever you like about neutrality's honesty."

"Goldmoon carries a staff that heals, which is either proof of forgotten gods or proof that magic doesn't need permission from any of them.
