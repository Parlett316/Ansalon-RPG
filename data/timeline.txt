# data/timeline.txt -- hand-authored canon character schedule for the
# chance-encounter system. Grammar: docs/TIMELINE_NOTES.md. All eight
# Heroes of the Lance share one schedule through Dragons of Autumn Twilight
# (Solace -> Haven/Darken Wood -> Xak Tsaroth -> Qualinesti -> Pax Tharkas
# -> Tarsis), then diverge at Tarsis as of Dragons of Winter Night: Sturm,
# Flint, and Tasslehoff continue on, first to Ice Wall Castle (a
# dragon-orb quest, reachable only by the sea-travel mechanic -- see
# GameState::hasBoat / docs/ARCHITECTURE.md) and then to the High Clerist's
# Tower, while Tanis, Raistlin, Caramon, Goldmoon, and Riverwind are swept
# east to Silvanesti instead, griffon-carried into a second dragon-orb
# crisis of their own -- see docs/TIMELINE_NOTES.md's "Dragons of Winter
# Night"/"Ice Wall"/"Silvanesti" sections for the sourcing. Original prose
# throughout, not quoted from the books. More
# locations are meant to be added the same incremental way zones were.
#
# CHARACTER <id>          starts a block
# NAME <text>              display name
# PRESENCE <location-id> <day-start> <day-end> <text>   one or more
# SAY <text>                optional, follows a PRESENCE line -- the
#                           default/first-meeting spoken line shown when
#                           the player presses 't' during that window
# SAY_IF <condition> <text> optional, zero or more; a reactive variant of
#                           SAY, checked in order the first time this
#                           window is talked to -- the first condition
#                           that matches the player's character wins over
#                           the plain SAY. Conditions: good, evil (align-
#                           ment's ethical axis), human/dwarf/elf/gnome/
#                           halfelf/kender (race), fighter/mage/
#                           cleric/thief/tinker (class)
# SAY_AGAIN <text>          optional; shown on every talk after the first,
#                           instead of the generic recognition fallback
# TOPIC "<label>" <text>    optional, zero or more; if any exist, a topic
#                           picker follows the greeting -- label is quoted
#                           (see docs/TIMELINE_NOTES.md)
# END                      closes the block

CHARACTER tanis
NAME Tanis Half-Elven

SUBJECT halfelf,heritage,elf,human Tanis's easy half-smile turns rueful. "I'm half-elf, in case the ears and the beard didn't already give it away -- half of two things and, most days I met before this journey, fully welcome to neither one." He shrugs, an old practiced motion. "Qualinesti raised me anyway. I owe them that, even on the days I still feel like a guest in my own childhood home."
SUBJECT leader,leadership,responsibility Tanis exhales, the particular tired half-smile of a man who never applied for the job. "Somewhere between Solace and here, everyone just started looking to me when a decision needed making. I don't remember agreeing to that. I don't seem to get a vote in un-agreeing to it either." A pause. "I count heads more than I used to. I'd rather have too many people to worry about than too few."
SUBJECT caramon Tanis's expression warms, plain and uncomplicated in a way it rarely is otherwise. "Caramon's the steadiest sword-arm I've ever stood beside, and not nearly as simple as he lets people believe. He carries more than his brother on that back of his -- he carries most of us, some days, and never once complains about the weight where we can hear it." A faint smile. "I've stopped trying to talk him into resting. He wouldn't know what to do with the time."
SUBJECT goldmoon Tanis's tone turns careful, respectful. "Goldmoon walked into the Inn a chieftain's daughter with a blue staff and a great deal to prove, and she's spent every day since actually proving it. I watched five years of searching for the true gods end the night she arrived, and I still don't think she's caught up to what that means for her yet." A pause. "I don't envy the weight of it. I trust her to carry it anyway."
SUBJECT riverwind Tanis considers a moment before answering. "Riverwind doesn't say much, and everything he does say is worth the wait. He walked into the Forsaken Lands alone for a promise most men would've talked themselves out of, and came back with the one thing in this whole company I'd call miraculous without qualifying it." A faint, respectful nod. "I trust his read on danger over almost anyone's. He's earned that the hard way."
SUBJECT tasslehoff,tas Tanis's expression softens into something between exasperation and real fondness. "Tasslehoff will empty your pockets, misplace your patience, and then do something so recklessly brave three minutes later that you forget to be angry about either one." A rueful headshake. "I've given up counting how many times that kender's curiosity has nearly gotten him killed, and how many times it's saved the rest of us instead. The math never quite balances, and I've stopped expecting it to."
SUBJECT_WHEN 0 159 sturm Tanis's expression turns fond, if a little exasperated. "Sturm holds himself to a Code most of us gave up trying to live by years ago, and somehow makes the rest of us feel obligated to try harder anyway just by standing near him." A faint smile. "He's exactly as stubborn as he looks, and exactly as honest. I trust both of those more than I trust most people's cleverness."
SUBJECT_WHEN 160 -1 sturm Tanis is quiet a moment before he answers, something unhealed in it. "Sturm stood alone on that wall and made himself the target so the rest of us could finish what we needed to finish. Knighted five days, dead a hero -- I keep turning that trade over and I still don't know if I'd have had the nerve to make it myself." A pause. "I miss arguing with him. I didn't expect that to be the part I'd miss most."
SUBJECT_WHEN 0 167 raistlin Tanis's expression turns careful, weighing something. "Raistlin and I don't trust each other the way friends are supposed to, and somehow I still trust him with my life more readily than men who'd call themselves my friends outright." A pause. "He's never once let me see what it costs him to keep helping us. I've stopped asking. I don't think he'd tell me the truth even now."
SUBJECT_WHEN 168 -1 raistlin Tanis's jaw tightens. "He knew what waited for us across that river before we ever crossed it, and he still hasn't told me how, or what he traded for the knowing." He's quiet a moment. "Now he's gone off to Palanthas alone, and nobody's had word since. I don't like not knowing whether to be worried or just used to it by now. With Raistlin, it's usually both."
SUBJECT_WHEN 0 191 flint Tanis's mouth curves, warm and genuine. "Flint's been growling at me since before I could grow this beard, and I've never once doubted where I stood with him because of it. A dwarf who tells you exactly what he thinks, whether you asked or not, is worth ten who only tell you what you want to hear." A fond shake of the head. "I'd trust that gruff old dwarf with anything that actually matters."
SUBJECT_WHEN 192 -1 flint Tanis is quiet for a long moment. "Flint fell doing exactly what he always did -- finding the way forward before anyone else thought to look, and not making a fuss about it after." His jaw works. "I've known him longer than I've known almost anyone alive. I keep expecting to hear him grumbling about something just out of sight, and then remembering I won't."
SUBJECT_UNKNOWN Tanis gives you a puzzled, apologetic half-smile. "I'm not sure I follow. Ask me something I've actually got an answer for -- I've got precious few of those today, but I'll do what I can with the ones I have."

CHARACTER raistlin
NAME Raistlin Majere

SUBJECT caramon,brother Raistlin's gaze drifts, briefly and unguarded, to the broad-shouldered man working through a mountain of food at the corner table. "My twin. My other half, if you credit that sort of thing." The half-smile that follows has real warmth buried under the dryness. "He worries over me the way some men worry over a favorite hound -- loudly, constantly, and mostly without being asked. I have told him it does neither of us any good." A pause. "He has never once listened. I confess I would not know what to do with him if he started."
SUBJECT magic,test,towers,sorcery,tower Raistlin's thin hand drifts, briefly, to his own hooded face. "I passed the Test, at the Towers of High Sorcery. I will not describe to you what it cost -- you have not earned that answer, and I find explaining myself to the uninitiated tiresome." His golden eyes glitter, sharp with old, private pain. "I will say only that I paid for this in a currency I expect to go on missing for the rest of my life. It was, on the whole, a fair trade."
SUBJECT kitiara,kit,sister,half-sister Raistlin's expression cools by several careful degrees. "Our half-sister. A complicated word for what she has actually become to us, but I have yet to find a better one." A thin, humorless smile. "Kitiara answers to whichever banner currently profits her most -- I do not pretend to know which one that is today, only that she will have already worked out the exchange rate before she chooses it." His golden eyes flick away. "Ask Caramon, if you want someone who still hopes for a different answer. I stopped some years ago."
SUBJECT eyes,hourglass,pupils,sight Raistlin's golden eyes glitter, distant for a moment. "The Test left me these -- hourglass pupils, so that I might see time itself, the way it wears away everything it touches." His thin mouth curves, humorless. "I see you dying, slowly, by inches, the same as I see everyone. It does not make company more pleasant. It has, admittedly, made me considerably harder to lie to."
SUBJECT skin,gold,golden Raistlin holds one thin, golden-hued hand up to the firelight without being asked, turning it as though he still finds the color a mild curiosity. "A parting gift from the Towers, along with the eyes. It does not wash off, in case you were wondering, and no, I have stopped minding the way people stare. Mostly." A thin smile. "It has its uses. Very little about me reads as harmless anymore, and I have found that worth quite a lot."
SUBJECT wayreth,conclave Raistlin's expression turns momentarily distant. "The Tower of Wayreth does not stay where it was the day before -- it moves through the forest at its own pleasure, and finds you rather than the reverse, if the Conclave judges you worth finding at all." A dry note creeps into his voice. "Three orders, one Tower, and a great many old wizards who would each prefer their own robe's color ran the world. I have found it wiser to let them believe they might."
SUBJECT staff,magius Raistlin's hand finds the plain-looking staff never far from his side, something almost fond in the gesture. "Par-Salian's own parting gift, given the day I passed the Test. It looks like a beggar's walking stick, which I have found to be its own kind of protection -- very few men draw a blade on a cripple leaning on a stick, right up until they regret it." His golden eyes glint. "I would not travel far from it, if I were you. I certainly don't."
SUBJECT par-salian,parsalian,salian Raistlin's voice cools, respect and old resentment tangled together in it. "Par-Salian is head of my order, and the one who judged me fit to attempt the Test at an age when most mages are still copying their masters' scrolls by candlelight. He warned me what it might cost. I do not believe, even now, that either of us fully believed the number he named." A thin pause. "He was not wrong to let me try. I have not yet decided whether he was right."
SUBJECT robes,order,orders,neutral,neutrality Raistlin's red robes shift as he inclines his head, very slightly. "White for the well-meaning, black for the ones who stopped pretending, and red for those of us who decided the argument between them wasn't worth having." His thin smile doesn't warm.
